Which mesh, and when

Four kinds of screen do almost every job in Florida. Here is what each one is for, what it adds to the price, and how to tell whether your cage needs a panel or the lot.

Repair or rescreen?

Press a thumb into a panel that looks fine. If the mesh feels dry and papery, or a corner tears when you push, it is brittle and the rest is not far behind. If more than a third of the panels are torn or brittle, a full rescreen is cheaper per panel and the mesh will all age together.

If the frame is sound and most panels still give and spring back, repair the torn ones and keep your money. Jerry will tell you which it is from the photos, and he will say so if a repair is the better deal.

Before June

Loose panels and rusted anchors are what fail first in a storm. A spring check takes an hour and is cheaper than a cage on the pool deck in September.

  • Anchor bolts: rust streaks at the base of a post mean they are going. Replace in the same hole.
  • Screws along the roof beams: any that spin or are missing.
  • Panels that flap: a loose panel pulls the spline out of its neighbours.
  • Doors: a door that does not latch will swing in the wind and tear the panel beside it.
